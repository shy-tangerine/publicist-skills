# Brazil newsroom ethics and source strategy

> Sources: FENAJ, 2007-08-01; FENAJ, Unknown; Ajor, 2024-02-16

## Overview

A Brazilian pitch should make verification easier and preserve editorial independence. The publicist's client interest is not a substitute for truth, public interest, privacy, source protection, or conflict disclosure.

## Evidence and independence

The FENAJ code grounds journalism in accurate information, factual truth, public interest, careful verification, privacy, and source confidentiality. It also addresses conflicts between press-office work and newsroom coverage. A publicist should therefore identify the represented organization, disclose commercial relationships, preserve the scope of evidence, and avoid language that suggests an independent endorsement.

Never fabricate a quote, customer, metric, exclusive, embargo acceptance, or journalist interest. Label paid or sponsored routes. Do not offer benefits for coverage, impersonate a reader or reporter, or suppress material limitations.

## Source preparation

Prepare each spokesperson with verified credentials, the narrow scope they can address, availability, conflicts, methodology, limitations, and response to a plausible critical question. Confirm whether statements are on the record, background, or confidential before the interaction; an agent cannot promise anonymity or bind a journalist to an embargo.

Source diversity is editorially valuable, but a directory's purpose and access rules still control use. Ajor notes that some source banks are organized by gender, race, topic, or region to diversify interviewees and decentralize narratives. Use that insight to improve representation, not to create an indiscriminate outreach list.

## Corrections and follow-up

Provide a correction path and respond quickly to a substantiated error. A follow-up should add useful information and require fresh approval. Stop after an opt-out, complaint, identity conflict, expired opportunity, or unresolved risk to a source.
