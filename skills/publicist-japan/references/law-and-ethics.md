# Japan law and ethics guardrails

## Personal information and cross-border handling

The Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI) regulates personal information and third-party provision. A journalist’s publicly displayed business contact is not a blanket permission to collect, enrich, export, or retain it. Minimize fields, record purpose and provenance, retain only as long as needed, secure access, and honor correction/deletion or opt-out requests where applicable. Sending a contact record or using an overseas CRM may be a cross-border transfer or processor issue; identify roles, notice/consent and safeguards with counsel before doing so. Never infer sensitive attributes.

## Email and platform rules

The Act on Regulation of Transmission of Specified Electronic Mail is relevant to unsolicited commercial email. Determine whether a message is commercial, the sender/identity and opt-out requirements, and whether an exception applies; do not assume earned-media outreach is exempt. Provider rules control PR-wire acceptance, prohibited claims, impersonation, and paid placement labeling. `robots.txt` is not authorization; use licensed APIs, manual public pages within terms, or user-provided data. No scraping or bulk messaging.

## Transparency and editorial independence

Disclose the client, commercial relationship, sponsorship, affiliate links, and material conflicts. Do not offer payment, gifts, or guaranteed coverage disguised as earned media. A release-wire publication is distribution, not independent editorial endorsement. PRSJ guidance emphasizes responsible, truthful PR; the Japanese Psychological Association guidance shows why research releases should state methods, limits, and avoid overclaiming.

## Escalate

Return `NEEDS_REVIEW` for uncertain legal basis, consent, sensitive data, cross-border vendor, club credential, embargo, election/crisis topic, sponsored content, correction dispute, or any request to hide identity or bypass access. Ask qualified Japanese privacy/communications counsel; do not state a definitive legal conclusion.
