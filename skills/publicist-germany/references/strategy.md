# Germany strategy

## Channel selection

Start with the smallest credible set: a regional newsroom for a local consequence, a `Fachmedium` for specialist proof, and a national desk only for a broad consequence. Use IVW or publisher data as a reach signal, then verify `Ressort` fit through recent reporting. Include podcasts, newsletters, and trade publications only when their audience and submission route are evidenced.

## Startup and expert positioning

A startup should offer a concrete German customer problem, independently checkable evidence, founder expertise, and access to a customer or neutral expert when permission exists. An expert should define a narrow beat, explain what can be said on or off record, give realistic availability, and disclose employer and commercial ties. Avoid translated hype, unsupported market-size claims, and claims such as “market leader” without proof.

## Timing and follow-up

Respect the request deadline and newsroom workload; give a reported story enough lead time and state any embargo explicitly. A follow-up should add new information or clarify availability, not repeat the same pitch. Treat it as a new external action and show the recipient, channel, timing, and exact copy immediately before sending.

## Criticism resilience

Seek counter-evidence and a plausible opposing view before drafting. Assume the journalist may test every claim and ask who benefits. Prepare methodology, limitations, a correction route, and a German-speaking contact. Mark changing legal, survey-quality, customer-consent, or platform-rule questions `NEEDS_REVIEW`.
